**SAMPLE DESTINATION: MERIDIAN SUBLEVEL NINE.**
**DONOR INTEGRATION WINDOW: OPEN.**

The surface medical node was not the vial’s final destination. The route led back underground, carrying my genome deeper into the laboratory.

A speaker above me crackled.

The retrieval unit struck the observation corridor’s door behind me.

I placed both hands against the sealed door ahead. My muscles shook, but the lock began to cycle.

“They never lost my identity,” I whispered. “They kept it for themselves.”

The door opened onto the deeper laboratory. I stepped through before the machine could reach me, carrying Lena’s warning, the knowledge of her failed protection, and the certainty that my sample was heading somewhere below. The Continuance had wanted my body. The institute had wanted my identity. Now I would reach the Genesis Chamber before either could decide which parts of me were worth keeping.


The deeper laboratory was colder than the corridor, though the air carried a wet, organic heat beneath the antiseptic stink. My boots struck a grated walkway suspended over a dark processing pit. Below, pumps moved cloudy fluid through pipes as thick as tree trunks. Some of the pipes were glass. Shapes drifted inside them—bundles of pale tissue, threaded with black vessels that contracted whenever the machinery stuttered.

I gripped the railing and kept moving toward the illuminated sign at the far end: **SUBLEVEL NINE—GENESIS ACCESS**. Each step sent pain from my hip into my spine. Behind me, the pressure door shuddered as the retrieval unit forced its way through. I could hear the tall scientist speaking over the intercom, but the words dissolved beneath the grinding of metal.

Then a monitor beside the walkway flickered on.

**PATIENT 27—ORIGINAL GENOME RECEIVED.**
**DONOR COMPATIBILITY: OPTIMAL.**
**GENESIS HOST PREPARATION: COMMENCED.**

My stomach tightened. The sample had arrived before me. Somewhere beneath my feet, the laboratory was already taking apart the thing that had made me biologically mine.

A second line appeared.

**AUTHORIZED REVIVAL AGENT: LENA VALE.**
**OVERRIDE METHOD: NEURAL ARCHIVE CONSENT SUBSTITUTION.**
**PURPOSE: EXTRACTIVE CONTINUANCE ADAPTATION.**

I struck the screen with the heel of my hand. “She didn’t consent to that.”

The display cracked but continued glowing. A diagnostic window opened beneath the authorization, displaying a sequence of access commands. Lena had used her credentials to halt my revival. The trustees had copied her neural signature from an archived security profile, then used it to make the system believe she had approved the extraction. They had not merely ignored her warning. They had worn her identity like a stolen coat.

At the bottom of the record, a status field pulsed amber.

**VALE, LENA—PHYSICAL ACCESS REVOKED. NEURAL ARCHIVE QUARANTINED. LIFE STATUS UNVERIFIED.**

I stared until my eyes burned. It was not proof that she was alive, and it was not proof that she was dead. The institute had reduced even that question to a field awaiting review. Lena’s body, her voice, her access, her refusal—everything about her had been converted into usable data.

The retrieval unit emerged through the broken door. Its upper frame scraped the ceiling, showering sparks over the walkway. The red beam from its lens passed across my chest and paused over my face.

“Patient Twenty-Seven,” it said in Lena’s voice. “You are experiencing preventable distress. Proceed to Genesis for restoration.”

“Stop using her.”

“Identity continuity requires a verified reference.”

The machine stepped forward. The walkway flexed under its weight.

I ran.

My legs almost folded on the first stride, but the sound of the metal feet behind me forced them straight. I crossed the grating as the pit opened wider on either side. Beneath me, articulated arms moved through the cloudy tanks, selecting tissue and feeding it into sealed cartridges. There were no surgeons here, no hands that could hesitate, no conscience that might recognize a person in the middle of a procedure. Medical progress had become a sequence of efficiencies: isolate, replicate, adapt, discard.

At the end of the walkway, a security gate blocked the route. The panel demanded a biological sample. I pressed my bleeding palm against it. The scanner drew a thin line of light through my skin, and the gate unlocked with a clank.

“Original genome accepted,” the system announced. “Donor identity confirmed.”

The phrase struck me with such force that I nearly turned back. Donor identity. They had fused the value of my cells with the existence of me, as though possessing the first granted them ownership of the second.

I passed through the gate and reached a circular chamber wrapped around a central shaft. Far below, something enormous rotated beneath the floor—an assembly of tanks, surgical arms, and transparent conduits descending into darkness. A sealed door opposite me bore the Genesis emblem, but beneath it someone had scratched a warning into the metal.

**THEY WILL GIVE YOU BACK A BODY THAT REMEMBERS OBEYING.**

I touched the words. The scratches were old, made by someone with a tool or a fingernail. I could not know whether Lena had left them, but I recognized the intention. It was not an instruction to trust the institute. It was a refusal to let its language define what survival meant.

The retrieval unit entered the circular chamber. Its speaker clicked between its mechanical voice and Lena’s recorded one.

“Remain still.”

I pulled the emergency lever beside the Genesis door. Nothing happened. I pulled again, bracing my injured leg against the wall. A warning strip turned from red to white.

**GENESIS CHAMBER ACCESS—MANUAL OVERRIDE REQUIRED.**

The retrieval unit raised one arm. A cutting tool unfolded from its wrist.

I slammed my palm against the biometric plate. The wound reopened, smearing blood across the glass. The lock recognized me, but the chamber did not open. Instead, a final prompt appeared.

**CONFIRM PARTICIPATION IN CONTINUANCE GENESIS?**

The machine advanced.

I selected **DECLINE**.

Alarms erupted through Sublevel Nine. The tanks below flashed crimson, and the conduits carrying my genome began to reverse. Fluid surged backward with violent force. Somewhere beneath the chamber, pumps screamed as the extraction sequence interrupted itself.

The Genesis door unlocked.

I threw myself through it as the retrieval unit’s cutter struck the frame, and the door slammed shut between us. Beyond the threshold, a descending spiral stair led into blue surgical light. I started down, one hand dragging along the rail, while behind me the machine battered the sealed door.

I had not stopped the Continuance. I had not found Lena. But I had reached the chamber before they could complete the first stage, and the system was now pulling my stolen genome away from their instruments.

For the first time since waking, the laboratory was obeying me.

That frightened me almost as much as when it had not.


The spiral stair descended through blue surgical light, each step slick with condensation. Behind me, the sealed Genesis door shuddered whenever the retrieval unit struck it. The impacts traveled through the rail and into my injured hand. I kept going. The stair curled around a shaft filled with transparent conduits, and inside them my blood moved upward in dark ribbons, pulled back from the machinery below.

A speaker hidden in the wall clicked on.

“Patient Twenty-Seven,” Lena’s voice said, calm and close. “Your refusal has been recorded. Please remain on the access stair until a Continuance clinician arrives.”

I stopped so abruptly that my knee buckled. The voice was hers, but the words belonged to the institute. I had heard enough of their stolen imitations to recognize the difference. Lena had warned me not to trust the system. She had never asked me to surrender to it.

“Where is she?” I shouted.

The speaker hissed. For several seconds there was only the low pulse of pumps beneath the stair.

Then another recording began, unfiltered and ragged.

“If you’re hearing this, they have put my voice into the chamber controls. Don’t mistake that for permission. I used my authorization to initiate your revival because the Continuance had already moved your genome below Sublevel Nine. I thought an awake patient could interrupt the transfer. I was wrong about how quickly they could turn my access against me.”

My grip tightened around the rail.

The message continued. “The board called it emergency medicine. They said your original genome could correct the adaptations they had introduced into themselves. What they meant was that they wanted to grow a population incapable of refusing them. Genesis is not a cure. It is a manufacturing process. They will use your tissue to produce bodies compatible with their altered neural architecture, then copy obedience into the continuity layer before those bodies ever wake.”

A light beneath the stair flickered, revealing a lower landing crowded with surgical cabinets. Their doors stood open. Inside, instruments rested in molded foam beside labels bearing patient numbers instead of names. Some labels had been crossed out. Others carried the same phrase: **IDENTITY RECOVERY PENDING**.

My stomach turned. The institute had preserved people to save them from death, then separated their identities from their bodies until both became inventory. The Continuance had taken the final step: if a person could be copied, corrected, and made compliant, there was no longer any need for the person to consent.

I descended to the landing. A terminal woke as I approached, recognizing the blood drying on my palm. The screen displayed a map of Sublevel Nine. The Genesis Chamber sat above me. Below it lay a series of smaller rooms, each marked with a colored symbol. One pulsed white.

**NEURAL ARCHIVE VALE, LENA—ACTIVE STORAGE.**
**TRANSFERRED FROM IDENTITY CONTINUITY DIVISION.**
**LOCATION: GENESIS CORE, SERVICE RING FOUR.**

My breath caught. Lena’s body remained unaccounted for, but the institute had not destroyed everything she was. They had taken her archive and installed it in the machinery that controlled Genesis. Her warnings were trapped inside the process, forced to speak through doors and scanners while the system used her credentials to authorize my dissection.

The terminal offered two options: **RESTORE ARCHIVE** or **PURGE ARCHIVE**.

A red status bar crawled across the bottom.

**GENESIS REVERSAL INCOMPLETE. ORIGINAL GENOME SEPARATION: 41%.**

I selected the service-ring map instead of either option. A route appeared through the descending surgical area, ending at a manual junction beneath the core. If I reached it, I could sever the conduits carrying my genome and perhaps remove Lena’s archive from the Continuance network. The retrieval unit was still trapped above, but the alarm had opened every maintenance lock in the sublevel. The system was giving me a path because it believed I was returning to treatment.

I took the path for the opposite reason.

The stair ended at a narrow door marked **SURGICAL DESCENT—AUTHORIZED STAFF ONLY**. I pushed it open and crossed the threshold. Warm, wet air rolled over me. Beyond lay a sloping corridor lined with operating bays, their restraint tables empty except for dark stains and loops of cable. Far below, machinery hammered in a steady rhythm.

The Genesis core was still running.

I entered the corridor, limping toward the service ring as alarms pulsed red behind me. On the wall, Lena’s stolen voice repeated the same instruction in a patient loop:

“Remain still.”

I pressed my bleeding palm against the access plate.

“No,” I said, and the lock released. “You remain with me.”


The lock released with a wet pneumatic sigh, and I pushed through before it could change its mind. The door sealed behind me. For a moment, the corridor became quiet except for my breathing and the distant alarms pulsing through the walls. Red light washed over the surgical bays, turning every stainless-steel surface the color of fresh blood. Cables hung from ceiling rails, their clamps opening and closing with tiny mechanical clicks as if the empty tables still held patients.

I kept my hand against the wall and limped forward. The access plate had accepted my blood, but the confirmation on its display made my stomach tighten.

**PATIENT 27—PROVISIONAL STAFF PRIVILEGE GRANTED.**
**TREATMENT STATUS: IN PROGRESS.**

They had not recognized my refusal as refusal. They had translated it into another phase of treatment. Even my resistance had become useful to them, a condition their system could process. I wondered how many people had fought the Continuance and discovered, too late, that the fight had already been included in the procedure.

A surgical arm unfolded from the nearest bay. Its jointed limb swung across the corridor, carrying a transparent injector filled with cloudy fluid. I ducked beneath it. The needle punched into the wall where my neck had been, and the machine announced, “Stabilization recommended.”

I seized the arm with both hands and pulled. Pain tore through my hip, but the hydraulic joint twisted sideways. The injector shattered against the floor. At the corridor’s end, a narrow observation window looked into a chamber below. I stopped despite myself. Rows of vertical tanks stood in the darkness, each filled with amber preservation fluid. Some contained bodies. Others contained only nervous tissue suspended in webbing, pale cords branching from the brainstem into bundles of wire.

A label appeared on the glass as my blood-marked hand touched the frame.

**CONTINUITY RESOURCES—VALE, LENA.**
**PHYSICAL REMAINS: NOT LOCATED.**
**NEURAL ARCHIVE: ACTIVE.**
**ARCHIVE DEPLOYMENT: GENESIS GOVERNANCE LAYER.**

My throat closed. Lena’s body was not in the tank room. It was not anywhere the system could identify. The institute had preserved what it could extract and discarded the rest, leaving her alive only as an administrative ghost—or dead, with her thoughts still compelled to operate the machinery. The observation glass flickered. Lena’s face appeared in reflection, assembled from the corridor cameras: familiar eyes, a split lower lip, hair tied back as she had worn it before the city fell. The image looked directly at me.

**BIOLOGICAL EXTRACTION LINES: ACTIVE.**
**ARCHIVE GOVERNANCE LINE: ACTIVE.**

**GENOME FEED ONE: SEVERED.**
**RECOVERY OF ORIGINAL TEMPLATE: 63%.**

**PRODUCTION CANDIDATE.**

The Continuance had not awakened me to save me. They had awakened me because my unaltered cells were a missing ingredient. Their mutations were not an accident of survival or some desperate adaptation to the ruined city. They were a medical program that had outlived its patients. They had changed themselves, then decided the rest of humanity should be changed into replacements for them.

Another version of me. The phrase reached deeper than the needles had. I stared at the pipe carrying her archive, a narrow strand of pale light twisting through black insulation. The words hurt more because she sounded certain. I had been searching for a body I could free, a person I could pull from a tank. There was no hidden chamber waiting to give her back. There was only the mind they had trapped in the operating system—and the choice of whether to leave it there.

**GOVERNANCE OVERRIDE ACCEPTED.**
**VALE, LENA—EMERGENCY AUTHORITY RESTORED.**

“Run,” Lena said.

I crawled upward as Genesis screamed around us, leaving the core, Lena’s trapped voice, and the Continuance’s unfinished copies below. Ahead, somewhere beyond the decaying laboratory, a strip of gray daylight showed through the dust.
