The empty space after those sentences was harder to bear than an accusation. In the army, he had learned to make a report sound complete even when half of it was uncertainty. Now a tidy account could condemn someone who had helped the children, or absolve him before he knew what he had done.

Eastern pointed to the wrapped papers beneath her coat, then uphill. He nodded.

They followed the pharmacy wall to the junction and turned toward the hospital. Satellite cables sagged between concrete balconies; an air-conditioning unit dripped onto a heap of flattened cartons. At the hospital’s service entrance, Middle recognized the chipped green door by the fan-shaped patch of missing paint beside its handle. He stopped beneath the lintel to wipe his shoes. Leaving mud here would not explain the annex prints, but he disliked adding another confusion to the night.

Inside, fluorescent light flattened the corridor. A porter pushed a linen cart past them, saying something Middle could not read while the man’s face was turned away. Eastern answered with a small gesture toward the records entrance. Middle looked at her mouth automatically, bracing for sound, but she had not spoken. His relief embarrassed him. He wanted to hear her again. He also wanted enough distance from the recess to remain standing.

They reached the records room by the same corridor they had used earlier. Middle pulled a chair toward the table before taking the papers from Eastern. His wet fingers left a mark on the wrapping; he dried them carefully on the inside of his coat. She sat opposite him and opened the notebook where both could reach it.

He laid the carbon copy flat while she brought the receiving register alongside it. The original page resisted opening, its binding drawing the inner margin into shadow. He slid his phone beneath the lamp to light the fold.

At first the entries looked identical: eleven evacuees, his name, the annex. He checked each line instead of reading them as a sentence. On the original, a stroke in the cramped writing near the margin appeared longer than it did on the carbon. He leaned closer. It might have been another letter. It might only have been ink spread through damaged paper.

Eastern reached for the pencil, then paused until he looked up.

**Different wording?**

He wanted to say yes. A difference would give them somewhere to go, something other than his missing hours to examine.

**Not established,** he wrote. **The copy is faint here. Photograph both at the same angle.**

She positioned the pages while he held the light. This was work he could do without borrowing another fragment of her childhood. When his hand began to tremble, he set the phone against a stack of folders rather than forcing it steady.

Eastern moved the notebook into the clear space between them. Beneath **Evidence**, she added: **Margin not yet legible enough to compare.**

Middle checked the photograph, enlarged it, and began again at the first line.


Eastern held the register open beneath the borrowed lamp while Middle enlarged the photographs on his phone. The records room seemed to contract around the table. Damp had lifted the paint in pale blisters, and the ceiling fan turned without cooling anything, its loose chain tapping against the metal housing. Somewhere beyond the corridor, a trolley struck a door. Eastern saw the vibration travel through the glass in the cabinet, but Middle did not look up. His attention remained fixed on the two pages.

She placed a sheet of transparent plastic over the carbon copy and marked the edges with a pencil. Middle watched her, then pointed to the original. He drew the same corner on the plastic, aligning the torn fibers rather than the printed lines.

“You’re right,” she said, keeping her voice low. “The pages have shifted. If we compare them by the writing alone, the margin won’t line up.”

His eyes lifted to her mouth. The change in his face was slight but unmistakable: the guarded stillness giving way to what looked like the shock of receiving her voice. Eastern felt the old unease return. Her voice was not supposed to cross the years between them. It had entered his silence in the annex, and every time she spoke now, the hospital seemed to bend toward that impossible passage.

She wrote the instruction in the notebook instead. **Align physical edges first. Then compare lines.**

Middle nodded. He held the original flat with one hand and used the other to slide the carbon beneath the plastic. The pages had different histories of damage. The original had been folded inward; the carbon had a water stain shaped like a thumb. When the edges matched, the suspected long stroke in the margin moved with the paper. It was not an added word. It was the lower curve of a letter obscured by the fold.

Eastern leaned closer. “Can you see it?”

Again, he looked at her. She regretted speaking, then understood that regret belonged to the cost of the miracle, not to him. He touched the notebook.

**I hear you.**

The words were simple, but her throat tightened around them.

He added, **Not the fan. Not the corridor. Only you.**

Eastern read the sentence twice. Despite what she had observed in the annex, she had caught herself wondering whether his hearing had returned in fragments, whether the hospital’s machinery reached him through some pathway no one understood. The answer, written between the register and its copy, was more frightening. Her voice was not merely the sound he had regained. It seemed to be the key that turned something locked inside him.

She forced herself back to the evidence. “Then I’ll speak only when you ask.”

Middle shook his head and wrote beneath his statement: **Not only silence. Need to know why.**

The pencil point broke. Eastern fetched another from the cup beside the rusted stapler and placed it in his hand. He did not thank her with a gesture; he simply continued, which felt more respectful than gratitude. Together they examined each line. The number eleven appeared in both entries. The receiving notation matched. His name, written in a clerk’s hurried hand, carried the same blot at the end. Beside the destination, the carbon showed a clean horizontal mark. On the original, the mark disappeared beneath a crease.

Eastern photographed the pages again, this time with the plastic sheet in place. She adjusted the lamp, then switched it off and used the phone’s narrow beam at an angle. The hidden indentation became visible: not a legible word, only the pressure of writing transferred through the sheet. It resembled a descending stroke, perhaps the end of a place-name, perhaps nothing more than the clerk’s hand slipping.

She wrote, **Physical impression confirmed. Meaning not confirmed.**

Middle tapped the sentence once, approving the distinction. Then he turned to the photograph from the annex. The scratches beneath the boarded window filled the phone screen. He placed the register beside it and drew a line between the margin and the wall.

His finger stopped over the deepest groove.

Eastern thought she understood what he was asking. “You think the mark on the page could tell us where the children went?”

He watched her carefully. She waited until he gave a small nod.

“But it might only tell us where the clerk thought they went,” she said. “That still wouldn’t prove they arrived there.”

He wrote, **And it would not prove I returned.**

The sentence opened the room’s coldest space. Eastern wanted to reach across the table, but she kept both hands visible on the notebook. His agency mattered more than her impulse to reassure him. She turned to a clean page and made three headings: **Document**, **Memory**, **Inference**.

Under **Document**, she entered the identical lines and the confirmed pressure mark. Under **Memory**, she wrote her recollection of his promise and her waiting, then his recollection of leaving. Under **Inference**, she left a blank.

Middle took the pencil and filled it with one sentence: **The margin may contain a destination connected to the annex.**

Eastern looked at him, then added, **It may also contain nothing useful.**

He considered the correction and nodded. Their trust no longer seemed to depend on agreeing quickly. It depended on allowing doubt to remain visible between them.

A voice called from the corridor, muffled by the records-room door. Eastern turned. The night clerk’s footsteps passed without stopping. Middle kept his gaze on the register. She pointed to the door, then to him, asking whether he wanted to leave. He shook his head and pulled the lamp closer.

At the bottom of the carbon copy, beneath the official entry, a faint line of pressure crossed the paper diagonally. It did not match the margin mark. Eastern placed the transparent sheet over it and saw that the line ran to the edge of the page, as though someone might have written on a larger form above it.

Middle noticed her expression. She gave him the pencil instead of speaking.

**Another impression. We need to look for a sheet that might have lain above this one before we decide what caused it.**

He covered the carbon copy carefully, not as a conclusion but as protection. Then he unlocked the lower drawer of the records cabinet and began searching for a form that might account for the impression. Eastern held the lamp over his shoulder, keeping the light where he needed it, while the hospital’s darkness gathered just beyond the circle they had made.


The lower drawer caught against his knee. Middle shifted his weight and pulled again, carefully enough that the folders stayed upright. Eastern lowered the borrowed lamp beside his shoulder. Its heat reached his cheek before the light reached the drawer’s back corners, where curled forms stood between cardboard dividers.

He searched by size first. The diagonal impression extended beyond the carbon’s printed border; a larger sheet might explain it. He passed over prescription slips and narrow laboratory requests, lifting each bundle beneath its lowest sheet. Years without hearing had taught him how easily damage happened outside his attention: a sleeve dragging a cup, a chair catching someone’s foot. Paper tore silently, too.

Near the back he found a broad form folded around several unused duplicates. A diagonal rule divided its lower corner from the main text. He stopped and held it toward Eastern.

She brought the lamp closer, then pointed to the table.

He laid the form beside the protected carbon. Across its top, in Arabic and smaller English lettering, were the words **Internal Movement—Porter Copy**. The diagonal corner held spaces for a receiving department and an acknowledgment. Middle felt his pulse quicken. A movement form was not a record of movement completed, but it was something they could test.

Eastern reached for the transparent sheet. He placed his hand over its edge, asking her to wait, and measured the slanted rule with the side of a blank envelope. The angle was wrong. When one end aligned with the pressure line, the other wandered away.

Disappointment came with an ugly force. He had already imagined the acknowledgment bearing his signature.

He wrote, **Not this sheet.**

Eastern examined the gap before answering in the notebook. **Same kind of form, perhaps. Different position?**

He almost shook his head. Instead he turned the envelope and tested the alignment again, allowing for a sheet laid crookedly over the carbon. The rule now followed the impression for part of its length, but the comparison established very little. Straight lines could be made to meet.

**Possible format. Not a match,** he wrote.

She added a question mark beside her suggestion. He looked at her hand resting beside his, close enough to touch but not claiming him. She had accepted his refusal without withdrawing her help. He slid the lamp toward her so she could inspect the paper herself.

“The lower receiving ward had a dispatch desk,” she said.

Her voice arrived with such clarity that he gripped the table. Behind her, the fan kept turning in absolute silence. For an instant he saw a white-painted rail under his own hand, though there was no rail in the records room. His stomach tightened around the image.

Eastern stopped speaking.

Middle kept his eyes open. Lamp. Folder. Water stain. Her sleeve.

The rail vanished. He wrote before the picture could acquire a story: **Brief image. White rail. No location established.**

His hand trembled over the last word.

“Do you want to stop?”

He heard concern, not an order. For years he had wanted voices back without remembering how a voice could enter him uninvited and leave him less certain of where he stood. Yet asking her never to speak would make her responsible for maintaining the wall around him.

He wrote, **Tell me about the desk. Slowly.**

She waited until he looked up.

“There was a rack for the porter copies. Beside the old lift. I saw it when we passed through the ward.”

No sudden recollection followed. Only her voice, and then its absence. He let his fingers loosen.

They photographed the broad form beside a ruler without placing it over the fragile carbon. Middle noted the drawer and divider, then returned it to its bundle. The register and carbon went back beneath their protective covering. He checked that the photographs showed the corners, the heading, the diagonal rule. They would look for a form with the same layout, not force this one to explain the mark.

He pointed toward the door, then wrote, **The rack first. No conclusion about the children.**

Eastern nodded and took the notebook.

He locked the drawer and tested the handle. Then he unplugged the lamp, letting its shade cool while he gathered his phone. Eastern opened the records-room door. Middle followed her into the corridor, where a cleaner was pushing grey water toward a drain. He waited for the mop to clear their path.

They passed the service entrance. Beyond its wired glass, wet backstreets shone beneath shop signs and tangles of balcony cables. A scooter’s headlamp swept the opposite wall; he heard nothing. He watched until it passed.

At the lower receiving ward, he pushed through the swollen door with his shoulder. Eastern steadied it behind him. Beside the disused lift stood a metal rack, its upper compartments empty, its lowest slot packed with folded papers.

Middle crouched without touching them. He photographed the rack as they had found it, then held out his hand for the notebook. This time, before searching, he made a place to record exactly what they took out.


Eastern kept one hand on the swollen door until it settled into its frame. The lower receiving ward smelled of wet plaster, iodine, and the sour dust that rose from the disused lift shaft. Water had collected in a shallow depression beneath the rack, reflecting the green emergency bulb in broken strips. Middle crouched before the lowest compartment with his phone held ready, its camera aimed at the papers but not yet touching them. He gave her the notebook.

She wrote at the top of a clean page: **Item number. Description. Rack position. Condition. Photograph before and after.** Beneath it, she added, **No conclusion until comparison is complete.** Middle read the list and tapped the last line. Eastern took the gesture as agreement rather than impatience, and the small recognition steadied her more than reassurance would have.

He photographed the rack from the front, then from the side where the lift’s flaking white paint showed behind it. Eastern noticed the rail-like strip immediately: not a rail, only the lift’s lower guard, painted white long ago and worn gray at the edges. She pointed to it, careful not to speak. Middle followed her finger and looked from the guard to the image on his phone. He wrote, **Observed object. Source of image unconfirmed.** She nodded. They did not turn the resemblance into a memory.

Middle photographed the first bundle in its slot, then removed it. Eastern watched his fingers pause above a folded sheet with a red thread tied around it. He looked at her, and she pointed to the notebook. He set the bundle on the floor, photographed it again, and wrote: **1—red-thread bundle, forms folded in half, dry, lower slot, outer sheet blank.** They opened it only after the photograph. The papers were meal-service tallies, each marked with a ward number and a series of initials. Nothing carried the diagonal rule. Middle returned the bundle exactly as he had found it.

The second item was a narrow envelope swollen by damp. Its flap had fused to the back, and the paper had the soft, granular texture of something once soaked. Eastern recorded the condition while Middle photographed both sides. Inside were three duplicate transfer slips, all bearing a straight horizontal line beneath the receiving-department field. One corner had been torn away. Middle held the top slip against the protected transparent sheet they had brought from the records room. The printed lines aligned, but the diagonal pressure impression did not. He shook his head and wrote, **2—transfer slips, possible format, no diagonal match.** Eastern accepted the correction without offering a theory.

The third bundle resisted. A strip of cloth had been caught around the rack’s bent wire, and when Middle pulled, the whole compartment shuddered. Eastern reached to brace the upright. The metal trembled beneath her palm, sending a faint vibration through her wrist. Middle looked toward her mouth, waiting. She did not speak until he had turned fully.

“Hold the bundle while I free the cloth.”

His eyes remained on her for a breath, then he passed the papers into her hands. The weight was greater than she expected. She kept them level while he worked the cloth loose with a blunt pencil. When the last thread came free, he reclaimed the bundle and photographed the wire, the cloth, and the papers before writing the next entry. **3—gray cloth caught on rack, porter slips, upper sheet creased diagonally.** He lifted the sheet toward the bulb.

The crease was not a written impression. It had been folded, then flattened badly. Beneath it, however, the second page showed a faint slanting indentation running from the acknowledgment box toward the margin. Eastern felt the ward sharpen around them. Middle placed the transparent sheet over it, aligning the paper edges first. The pressure line followed the diagonal on the carbon for nearly its full length, breaking only where the page had been torn.

He did not write at once. Eastern saw his thumb press into the notebook’s spine, as though he were holding back the urge to name what they had found. She waited until he looked at her.

“This is a close physical match,” she said. “It supports the possibility that a form with this layout lay above the carbon. It does not prove what was written on it, or that the movement happened.”

Middle’s shoulders lowered. He wrote, **3—strong candidate. Need complete form, not only impression.** Then, beneath it: **Do not move the page from the ward until photographed.**

Eastern took the phone while he flattened the sheet beneath two clean envelopes. She photographed the crease, the diagonal indentation, the torn edge, and the rack position. In the margin of the notebook she recorded the timing only as **before leaving the ward**, refusing to invent more certainty than they possessed. Middle checked each image, enlarging the corners and the acknowledgment field. No name emerged. No destination did.

A soundless disturbance passed through the corridor: the glass in the service door quivered, and a shadow crossed the wired pane. Eastern turned, but no one entered. Middle had already placed the candidate slip back atop its bundle. He pointed first to the photographed rack, then to the notebook’s inventory. She understood: one more item, then they would preserve the arrangement and compare the records before opening anything else.

She offered him the pencil. “Your choice.”

He looked at her mouth, then at the remaining papers. After a moment, he wrote, **Continue. You speak when needed.** The sentence warmed her despite the cold ward. He had not surrendered the decision to her; he had made it himself, and trusted her to honor it.

Together they reached into the lowest slot. Eastern held the notebook open while Middle lifted the next bundle clear. Beneath it, a broad form showed a diagonal ruled corner and a blank acknowledgment line. She recorded the fourth item before either of them unfolded it.
